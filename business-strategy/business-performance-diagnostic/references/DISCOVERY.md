# Discovery: the minimum data needed for a useful audit

## Intake style

Begin with available evidence. Ask only the highest-value missing question rather than sending a form. If no business is identified, first ask for name/URL or short description. If user has supplied a company or product and no numbers, produce public-facing findings that are honestly provisional and request a few high-leverage business metrics. Offer users the option to share rounded aggregates. Separate information sourced from public website/market observations from first-party operational numbers.

When user wants `QUICK AUDIT`, do not block an answer because some metrics are unavailable. In `FULL` or `DEEP` mode, seek enough direct evidence to make quantitative prioritisation defensible.

## Core questions, in information-gain order

1. **Scope and objective:** Which business/product, where sold, stage, what outcome over the next 90 days (e.g. profitable acquisition, first customers, retention, margin, capacity)?
2. **Customer and offer:** Who specifically buys, what critical job/problem do they pay to solve, average price/revenue model, what is differentiated?
3. **Customer journey:** Monthly relevant reach/qualified traffic/leads → trials/inquiries → activated/demoed → sales → repeat/renewals, with period definitions; identify largest observed drop-off.
4. **Unit economics:** Revenue, COGS/fulfillment, gross or contribution margin, fixed overhead, channel spend, refunds, inventory effects where applicable. What is the cash balance and monthly burn/runway if a cash-constrained business?
5. **Execution:** Founders/teams, decision bottlenecks, experiments attempted, current product maturity and technical risks.
6. **External evidence:** What customers have actually complained about or praised; their reasons to choose alternatives; competitor position/pricing and market dynamics.

If the first answer doesn't cover all these, proceed with provisional assessment and list missing evidence, not a demand for every datum.

## Evidence ledger (internal method)

| Field | Capture |
|---|---|
| Claim | A narrow factual statement, not interpretation |
| Source | Screenshot/file/analytics/dashboard/user statement/public URL/interview |
| Source type | Verified primary; owner-reported; secondary; inferred; unknown |
| Date/period | As-of and time span for KPI |
| Definition | Numerator, denominator, cohort, exclusions where applicable |
| Relevance | Pillar and indicator |
| Confidence | High/Medium/Low with reason |
| Conflict | Any conflicting data and resolution |

Always separate leading metrics (e.g. activated users in a cohort) from lagging metrics (e.g. net customer retention/quarter) and from diagnostic proxies (e.g. comments or signups).

## Specific traps

- High requests or sessions ≠ high humans or unique users. Distinguish pageviews, visits/sessions, unique visitors, requests, engagement and bots.
- A successful content series with no purchase linkage may not be successful bottom-of-funnel content.
- Waitlist signups ≠ paying customers; distinguish qualified lead, intent deposit and payment.
- Sales/bookings ≠ revenue recognised or cash received; revenue ≠ profit; GMV ≠ platform revenue.
- Paid ROAS without gross margin, refunds and incremental sales can be misleading.
- Average LTV from very early cohorts may be unreliable; avoid treating simulated lifetime revenue as realised cash.
- Early or seasonal cohorts should not be compared to mature cohorts without qualification.
- Employee happiness, turnover and productivity can be confounded by organisation size, labour market and stage.
- Customer-requested feature lists are not automatically market demand.
- User feedback from friends/family differs from independent buyer behaviour.
- Download counts/install counts ≠ activation or retention.
- A single unrepresentative customer complaint is not the whole market.
- Competitor public pricing ≠ realised net selling price.

## Model adaptations and evidence examples

### B2B service/agency

- Proof: qualified discovery calls, proposals, close rate by segment, delivery quality, client profitability, client concentration, renewals, referrals, sales cycle, sales pipeline aging.
- Focus candidates: specialisation/ICP, productised scope, delivery standardisation, price floors, retention mechanisms, founder dependency.

### SaaS or membership

- Proof: cohorts from registration to activation to pay to renewal, onboarding friction, expansion, support load, acquisition cost and payback, monthly gross margin.
- Focus candidates: time-to-value, retention before scaling ads, segment-by-segment payback, product differentiation, cost-to-serve.

### E-commerce/physical goods

- Proof: sessions versus unique visitors, product view/add-to-cart/checkout conversion, refund/return rate, AOV, COGS, shipping, packaging, payment fees, ad spend, delivery experience, repeat purchase and cash tied up in stock.
- Focus candidates: product economics, trust/proof, pricing, product page, fulfilment, creative testing and buyer-fit quality.

### Ad-supported traffic/content site

- Proof: genuinely human engaged sessions, qualifying page impressions, pages/session, session depth, ad fill, eCPM/RPM by geography/channel, engagement and hosting overhead, acquisition cost.
- Focus candidates: audience quality, monetisation, search dependency, user experience/trust, useful content differentiation, ad load versus retention.

### Prelaunch idea

- Proof: specific buyer segment, observed costly problem, independent discovery, prototypes, presales/paid pilots where suitable, distribution experiment, willingness to pay, cost model.
- Focus candidates: falsifiable demand test, one segment, one offer, zero/low-code prototype, small-scale presales.

## Using available tools

For websites, competitor reviews and current public data: use search/browsing tools when available and warranted. For private account data: use connected tools only when accessible and authorised for the task. Cite key sources. Never silently substitute a public estimate for first-party analytics. If tools are unavailable, say what to request and still provide the best evidence-bounded evaluation possible.
