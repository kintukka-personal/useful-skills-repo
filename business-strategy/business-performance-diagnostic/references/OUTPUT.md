# Reporting contract and reusable templates

## Default full report

Use readable paragraphs/tables; adapt presentation to chat. No table is necessary for a short focus-only answer. Prefer concise, commercially literate explanations over overformatted filler.

### A. Executive assessment

**Business / scope / stage / model / evaluation date:**

**Verdict:** one paragraph describing the system's current performance without claiming audited certainty. Say how much evidence supports the view.

**Binding constraint:** one sentence with evidence and a causal hypothesis.

**What to focus on first:** one action and why it outranks other options.

**Risks:** financial, market, team, execution or legal/technical risk if supported.

### B. Five-pillar scorecard

| Pillar | Score or provisional | Confidence | What's working | Limiting weakness | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing | xx/100 or insufficient data | High/Med/Low | ... | ... | ... |
| Sales | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Team | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Technology/delivery | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Finance | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Show at least one evidence detail per pillar when available, and mark missing-data limitations in plain language. Disclose any excluded N/A indicators or stage-weight changes. Do **not** print a composite score when insufficient coverage.

### C. Strengths and gaps

**Protect and amplify:** up to three advantages backed by buyer/commercial metrics or observed execution evidence.

**Needs work:** up to three specific underperforming mechanisms with causal theories, evidence and a practical consequence.

### D. Root-cause / constraint map

`Audience → Acquisition → Activation → Sale → Retention → Margin → Reinvestment` (adapt to business).

- What is the clearest observed bottleneck?
- What alternative explanations exist?
- Why fixing other things first may produce little upside?
- What experiment can falsify the chosen diagnosis?

### E. Ranked work list

| Priority | Intervention | Why now / expected effect | Effort | Confidence | KPI and decision threshold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ... | ... | hours/days/weeks | ... | ... |
| 2 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| 3 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

For top 1, always add a mini execution card: Owner/role, hypothesis, prerequisites, exact steps, measurement plan, earliest data point, stop/continue conditions.

### F. 30/60/90-day roadmap

- **Days 1–30:** instrument baseline, validate #1 constraint and launch first test.
- **Days 31–60:** double down on evidence-backed wins, remove adjacent friction and implement one repeatable process.
- **Days 61–90:** decide whether to scale, change position/offer, improve retention, or stop wasteful activity.

Each phase: owner if known, deliverable, measurable proof and gate for next phase. Tailor timing to real resource constraints; do not promise outcomes.

### G. KPI scoreboard

| Metric | Current baseline | How calculated | Target/decision rule | Cadence | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If no baseline, print `Not yet measured` and specify instrumentation + discovery period. Do not invent a numerical target; may use a hypothesis target clearly identified as an experiment and subject to revision.

### H. Uncertainties and next action

Ask up to five high-information questions only if answering them will materially change advice. Conclude with **one next action that can be taken within seven days**.

## Lightweight quick-audit shape

**Verdict:** 2–3 sentences.

**Pillar snapshot:** five compact entries with `Strong / Developing / Weak / Unknown`, evidence/confidence and one finding; scores only where defensible.

**Binding constraint:** specific mechanism with alternative explanation.

**Top 3 actions:** immediate experiment + two follow-ons.

**Next 7 days:** owner, metric, stop/go criterion.

## Red-team report add-on

- **Three strongest reasons the current thesis might fail** (market, distribution, economics or delivery).
- **Counterevidence** that would change our mind.
- **Kill/rethink criteria**, scaled to risk and runway.
- **Opportunity cost** of pursuing the obvious but low-leverage work.

## Re-audit add-on

| Metric / pillar | Baseline period | Current period | Change | Comparable? | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

If period/definition changed, print `Not comparable` and describe the missing normalisation.

## Example excerpt (entirely hypothetical)

> **Business:** Example subscription software, early revenue. In the last 30 days, 1,000 appropriately qualified visitors, 100 trial starts and 10 paid conversions. Cohort retention and cost data not provided.
>
> **Likely immediate focus:** inspect trial activation and buyer objections before expanding marketing spend, **not** because 1% visitor-to-paid is automatically poor, but because there is no evidence yet of what prevents the other 90 trials from paying.
>
> **Marketing:** provisional; acquisition volume is visible, quality and source are unknown.
>
> **Sales:** provisional; trial-to-paid is 10%, but value realisation, customer fit and sales-led touchpoints unknown.
>
> **Finance:** unassessed; no COGS, acquisition spend, margin or runway given.
>
> **Experiment:** interview 8–12 recent non-converting trials, segment by activation events, change one onboarding step, compare same-definition conversion of new eligible cohorts, and decide whether to iterate or shift segment. Targets must be set from sufficient historical sample, not fabricated benchmarks.

That is the expected analytical discipline: use provided numbers when available, avoid overinterpreting them, and design a test capable of moving a real decision.
