# Five-pillar operating playbook (from the five screenshots)

This is the underlying philosophy, not a rigid or universally validated set of rules. Translate each into a diagnostic question and stage-appropriate action. Keep original distinctive ideas intact.

## 1. Marketing — The Netflix model

**Governing idea:** Run marketing like Netflix, not like a monolithic corporate brand. Audience segments respond to different categories; test formats until a repeatable series wins, then exploit the working format until results diminish.

**Three content funnels:**
- Top: entertainment — trends, team skits, day in the life, humour, personality; builds reach and awareness.
- Middle: education — storytelling, frameworks, explanations, breakdowns; converts viewers to followers and followers to leads.
- Bottom: product — direct demos, customer wins, how it works, proof; converts demand to buyers.

**Principles to test:**
- Build team brands and founder/operator visibility, not only a faceless company account.
- Build distribution before product completion; landing pages, early interest and waitlists can de-risk launch. Example 10,000 waitlist registrations in source is an anecdote, not a universal success criterion.
- Document the journey and actual value creation authentically; phone-quality media can beat overproduced media for trust, although this must be tested in the relevant audience.
- Media success is not commercial success: connect series to qualified leads, conversion or defensible brand outcomes.

**Diagnostic questions:** Is there a defined audience per series, systematic experimentation, repeatability, messaging consistency, full-funnel coverage, reliable attribution and meaningful prelaunch market signal?

## 2. Sales — Founder-led discovery and customer economics

**Governing idea:** Learn what customers buy, object to and value firsthand before outsourcing the sales function. Source's 'first $1M yourself' is a heuristic, not a prerequisite for every company.

**Principles:**
- Specific ideal customer profile: selling to everyone means serving no one well.
- Learn sales process, negotiation and objections with deliberate practice.
- Prune or restructure economically damaging customer engagements fairly, instead of sacrificing all resources to unsuitable buyers.
- Solve the underlying problem, not necessarily the feature the user requested.
- Prioritise onboarding and retention as well as acquisition.
- Price according to buyer value, segment, positioning, willingness to pay and unit economics; test premium approaches rather than automatically copying competitors or always raising prices.

**Diagnostic questions:** Can the business articulate buyer pains and objections? Does it know close/conversion rates and why it wins/loses? Do acquired customers reach value and return or renew? Is price supported by evidence and contribution margin?

## 3. Team — Ownership and high-performance culture

**Governing idea:** Organise like a high-performing sports team, with defined roles, standards, clear feedback and fair accountability, rather than implying family-like unconditional employment.

**Principles:**
- Seek independent ownership-minded talent; former founders can be valuable, but are not universally superior.
- Hire complementary exceptional people and delegate outcomes, not only tasks.
- Hire carefully, provide regular positive and constructive feedback, and manage sustained performance issues promptly and lawfully.
- Consider equity or incentive alignment if suitable; source examples include ESOP, four-year vest with a one-year cliff, share-count framing and '1M shares'. These are **not** general legal, tax, dilution, or incentive prescriptions; percentages and fully diluted ownership remain material.
- Run a monthly 'selfish hour' for employee growth, career or personal goals, without requiring the agenda to serve the business.
- Own unclear instructions and correct communication failures.

**Diagnostic questions:** Who owns outcomes? Is execution founder-dependent? Are roles clear? Can new talent onboard rapidly? Are incentives understandable and equitable? Is feedback constructive and recorded?

## 4. Technology — Prototype, validate, then engineer

**Governing idea:** Prototype cheaply and fast (source examples: Lovable or Cursor); observe real users, then invest in robust, secure, scalable development when the evidence and risk justify it.

**Principles:**
- Avoid vanity polish ahead of learning; logo/name perfection rarely proves the business.
- Avoid building a massive scalable platform before the product has users, while still satisfying baseline safety, security, compliance and reliability.
- Buy/use standard products for non-differentiating functions; build proprietary differentiation.
- Validate with independent users and ideally purchase/payment commitments—not only friends/family enthusiasm. Qualitative learning also matters before monetary commitment.
- Document building, run a focused landing page, collect interest and test real demand before major investment.

**Diagnostic questions:** What expensive assumptions remain untested? Is there an experiment cadence and user evidence? Are product investments linked to customer outcomes? Is engineering complexity appropriate for traction and technical risk?

## 5. Finance — Discipline, optionality and sound deal terms

**Fundamentals:** Outsource bookkeeping if useful, but never outsource understanding the P&L, cash, contribution margin and unit economics. Growth without acceptable economics can destroy value. Track cash runway, plan for breakeven or financing early, and follow lawful business/personal expense accounting.

**Capital:** Bootstrap when feasible to retain optionality; raise when it unlocks unusually valuable growth, speed or distribution. Do not treat fundraising as an automatic indicator of quality.

**Terms:** Evaluate liquidation preferences, seniority, participation, option pools, dilution and control. The slides say avoid >1x liquidation preferences and participating preferred; these are red flags for negotiation, not hard legal prohibitions or universally bad in isolation. Compare actual cash to founders under multiple exits, not just headline valuations.

**Legal:** Form suitable entities and document arrangements early; structure and employment/investment contracts matter, with qualified professional advice as needed. Source '10x cost later' is rhetorical, not a verified universal cost estimate.

**Diagnostic questions:** Can unit contribution be calculated by product, cohort and channel? Is gross margin healthy? Is cash conversion known? Is spend tied to measurable outcomes? Are cap table and terms understood? Are compliance needs addressed?
